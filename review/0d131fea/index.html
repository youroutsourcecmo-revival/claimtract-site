1 · THE IDEA

You know how to adjust. The hard part was always the next claim.

Good adjusters don't stall because they can't work a claim. They stall because the next homeowner doesn't find them in time. That's the part I built ClaimTract to fix: a website with your name and license, door tickets with your face and a video, articles that answer what homeowners search at 2 a.m., and follow-up that runs while you're on a roof.

Here's the idea. You open your own public adjusting firm, and it's 100% yours. I stay out of it. I don't own any of it and I don't run any of it. I keep building the software, and your firm uses it to find homeowners and to recruit adjusters.

2 · WHO DOES WHAT

You run the firm. I build the tools.

  • You: own the firm, adjust claims and lead it as the primary adjuster. The only thing standing between you and starting is a surety bond. Your license is already active.
  • Your firm: gives ClaimTract's free tools to Florida public adjusters and markets them. Adjusters who join your firm get the full suite, including the CRM, for $299 a month. Adjusters who stay outside pay $499.
  • Me: I build and run the software. ClaimTract earns only flat monthly software fees, never anything tied to a lead, a claim or a settlement.
  • The lawyers: I'll introduce you to Trujillo Vargas, the firm I worked with for years, to review all of this before anything is formed.

Florida caps public adjuster fees at 20% on most claims, and 10% on claims from a declared emergency during its first year. The fee comes in when the carrier pays, so a month's claims usually turn into money a month or two later.

4 · THE FIRST 90 DAYS

From your current job to your own firm

  1. Week 1–2: the paperwork. Get your surety bond. Trujillo Vargas reviews the plan and helps you set up the firm the right way.
  2. Week 2: you go live. Your website goes up with your name and license, and your Back office is ready on your phone.
  3. Week 3: doors. Door tickets go out, by you or a couple of people you hire. When I ran them, 50 tickets booked about 2 appointments, and that was before video.
  4. Month 2: claims. You sign your first claims. Every one you document turns into pages that keep bringing homeowners in.
  5. Month 2–3: the first checks. The undisputed payments start landing, and that's what funds the next round.
  6. Month 3: the team. You bring on your first adjuster, and your firm starts marketing the free software to recruit more.
5 · THE LEGAL SIDE

Nothing gets formed until the lawyers say it's clean

You should know this up front: my own Florida public adjuster license was revoked, under my former name, Gary Guichard. Florida law lets the state discipline a primary adjuster whose firm employs or contracts with someone whose license was revoked. That's your license on the line, not mine, and it's exactly why I'm keeping out of your firm completely.

If you have your own attorneys, use them too. I'll prepare a legal brief you can send them, with their own link to comment on every point, so this conversation starts on solid legal footing, not on my say-so.

Trujillo Vargas reviews everything first: whether your firm can use my software, how the pricing works, and anything else they spot. If they say no to any piece, we change it before you sign anything.
