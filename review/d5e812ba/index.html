SECTION 1

Ownership, control and the primary adjuster

Fla. Stat. §§ 626.8695, 626.8696, 626.8697

Under the proposal, Colin Phipps holds 51% of the Florida public adjusting firm and serves as its designated primary adjuster. Ezekiel Guichard holds 49% and separately owns 100% of ClaimTract, a software company that licenses software to the firm.

  • Who is listed with DFS. The firm license application must name each majority owner, partner, officer and director, and include fingerprints for each, unless the person is a currently licensed and appointed adjuster.
  • Governance. At 49%, what voting, consent, bank-account or operating rights can Ezekiel hold without becoming a person who "manages or controls" the firm, or creating a disclosure problem?
  • Prior revocation. Ezekiel previously held a Florida public adjuster license, under his former legal name, Gary Guichard, that was revoked. He has since changed his name legally to Ezekiel Guichard.
The central risk. Under § 626.8695(5), the Department may suspend or revoke the primary adjuster's license if the firm employs or contracts with a person whose license was denied, or is currently suspended or revoked. Under § 626.8697, the Department may also act against a firm on grounds involving its owners, managers or the people who control it. Colin's license is what's exposed here, which is why we're asking before anything is formed.
  • Primary adjuster duties. Colin would be accountable for adjusting done under his supervision and must provide services to the firm at all times, with a written plan for who steps in if he can't.

Questions for counsel

  1. Can Ezekiel hold any equity in the firm, given the revocation and § 626.8695(5)? If not, is 0% equity with only a software license the safe structure?
  2. Does a firm buying software from a company owned by Ezekiel count as "contracting with" him under § 626.8695(5)?
  3. If any equity is possible, what must be disclosed to DFS, and which rights must Ezekiel not hold?
SECTION 2

Flat software fees, in-house routing and solicitation

Fla. Stat. §§ 626.854, 626.8738

ClaimTract licenses software to the firm and to outside adjusters for flat monthly fees, never a percentage of claims and never per lead. Homeowners who reach out through ClaimTract content are routed only to adjusters appointed inside the firm, not to outside adjusters.

  • Referrals. § 626.854 restricts a public adjuster from paying anyone other than another public adjuster for referring business, and from accepting referrals from a person they do business with under any agreement to compensate that person.
  • Solicitation. § 626.854(1) defines a public adjuster to include anyone who, for money or anything of value, directly or indirectly solicits claims on behalf of a public adjuster. Acting as a public adjuster without a license is a third-degree felony under § 626.8738.
  • Where the two meet. ClaimTract owns pages that bring homeowners to the firm. If Ezekiel also shares in firm profits, could that be read as indirect solicitation for compensation, even though the software fee itself is flat?

Questions for counsel

  1. Is a flat monthly software fee, unrelated to leads or claims, clean under § 626.854?
  2. Does any profit distribution to Ezekiel change that answer?
  3. Who should own the homeowner-facing pages: the firm, or the software company?
SECTION 3

AI-drafted case studies, advertising review, privacy and departures

Fla. Admin. Code R. 69B-220.051

When an adjuster documents real claim work, ClaimTract drafts educational pages and local search content. If that adjuster later leaves the firm, the page stays up with the original author's name, and only the contact and routing details change.

  • Advertising review. Rule 69B-220.051 requires advertisements to include the licensee's full name and license number, and makes the named adjuster responsible for personally reviewing the content. Which generated pages are "advertisements", and what sign-off should Colin use?
  • Privacy. Names, addresses, claim numbers and photo location data are removed before any page is published.
  • Departures. Keeping the original author's name while changing who answers the phone avoids implying a former adjuster still works there. Is that enough?

Questions for counsel

  1. Do the pages count as advertising under Rule 69B-220.051?
  2. What disclosure belongs on a page written about one adjuster's claim after he leaves?

SECTION 5

What Ezekiel wants, an alternative structure, and anything we missed

Ezekiel's goal. Ezekiel wants no role in the firm's day-to-day operations. His aim is to build software for public adjusters.

An alternative we'd like you to weigh against 51/49:

  • Ownership. The firm is 100% Colin's. Ezekiel holds no equity and no role in the firm.
  • Colin's firm markets the software. As a condition of the arrangement, the firm runs outbound marketing of ClaimTract to Florida licensed public adjusters, wholesale. It operates the dialer, staffs the callers at its own cost, and works toward agreed targets for giving away the free tier. The firm benefits through adjusters who join it.
  • Pricing. Adjusters appointed with the firm get the full suite, including the CRM, for $299 a month. Adjusters outside the firm pay $499 a month.
  • Money flow. ClaimTract earns only flat monthly software fees. Nothing is tied to leads, claims or settlements.

Questions for counsel

  2. If the firm markets ClaimTract and ClaimTract pays the firm anything for that work, how should it be paid, if at all?
  3. Is a lower software price for adjusters who join the firm acceptable?
  4. What rules apply to the firm's outbound calls and texts to other licensed adjusters (consent, calling hours, registration for business texting)?
