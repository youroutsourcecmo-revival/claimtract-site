Can My Roofer or Contractor Handle My Insurance Claim?

THE SHORT ANSWER

Your contractor can fix your home and give you a repair estimate, but in Florida a contractor generally can't adjust your claim, interpret your policy or negotiate with the insurer on your behalf unless they also hold a public adjuster license. A licensed public adjuster works for you on the claim itself, while your contractor focuses on the repairs. Has a contractor offered to deal with your insurance company for you?

After a storm, contractors are often the first people at your door, and many offer to take care of the insurance side too. It sounds convenient when you're overwhelmed, but the person fixing your roof and the person representing your claim have different jobs, and Florida law treats them differently. Would it help to know where the line is?

A contractor's job is to repair your home and price the work. Their estimate can be valuable evidence for your claim, but Florida law generally prohibits contractors from adjusting a claim, interpreting your policy or advising you about your coverage unless they hold a public adjuster license. Has anyone asked you to sign something that lets them talk to your insurer?

There's also a conflict to think about. A contractor gets paid for the work they do, and their estimate is built around their own scope and prices, but the claim should cover everything the policy owes, including damage outside their trade, code upgrades and contents. Does your damage go beyond the roof or one part of the house?

Be careful with any offer that sounds too generous. Florida law restricts contractors from offering to waive or rebate your deductible or offering gifts to get you to file a claim, so an offer like that is a red flag, but a good contractor will simply give you a fair written estimate and let you handle the claim with the right help. Did anyone offer to cover your deductible?

The best setup is a team where everyone does their own job. A licensed public adjuster documents the full loss, reads your policy and negotiates with the insurer, while your contractor bids and does the repairs, but you stay in control of both relationships and the decisions. Would you like to talk about how that would work on your claim?

What to do next

  1. Get a written repair estimate from your contractor.
  2. Don't sign anything that hands your claim or your insurance benefits to someone else without reading it carefully.
  3. Be wary of any offer to waive or pay your deductible.
  4. Verify any adjuster's license with the Florida Department of Financial Services.
  5. Keep your contractor and your claim paperwork in separate folders.

When you're tired and the house is a mess, letting one person handle everything is tempting. Many homeowners end up with a claim built around one trade's estimate and miss everything else, but you can have a contractor you trust and a licensed adjuster working for you at the same time.
