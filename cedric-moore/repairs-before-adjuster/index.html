Should I Make Repairs Before the Insurance Adjuster Comes?

THE SHORT ANSWER

Make temporary repairs that stop more damage, like tarping a roof, shutting off water or drying out wet areas, and keep every receipt. Hold off on permanent repairs until the damage is documented and the insurer has had its chance to inspect, unless waiting would make things unsafe or worse. Is water still getting in, or is the damage stable for now?

Waiting for an adjuster while your house is open to the weather is stressful. Most policies actually require you to protect the property from further damage, but there's a big difference between stopping the damage and fixing it for good, and getting that line right protects your claim. Would it help to know which repairs are safe to do right now?

Temporary repairs are the ones that keep things from getting worse: a tarp on the roof, plywood over a broken window, shutting off the main water valve, pulling out soaked carpet and running fans. Your policy usually expects these, and the reasonable cost is typically part of the claim, but you need photos before and after and every receipt to get paid for them. Have you taken photos of the damage before touching anything?

Permanent repairs are different. Replacing the roof, the drywall or the flooring before the insurer inspects can make it harder to prove what was damaged and how badly, but documenting thoroughly first, with photos, video and measurements, keeps the evidence even after the repair. Has the insurer told you when its adjuster will come out?

Mold is the reason not to wait too long on water damage. Wet drywall and insulation can start growing mold within days in Florida's heat, so drying out and removing soaked materials is part of protecting the home, but keep a sample piece and photos of what you removed so the damage is still on record. Is anything in the house still wet?

If the insurer is slow to inspect and you can't safely wait, write down that you asked and when. Florida requires insurers to acknowledge and act on claims within set timeframes, but a written record of your requests protects you if they later question why you repaired before they came. Would you like help putting together a damage folder today?

What to do next

  1. Photograph and video every damaged area before any cleanup.
  2. Make temporary repairs only: tarp, board up, shut off water, dry out.
  3. Keep every receipt and note the hours you spent.
  4. Save samples of removed materials when possible.
  5. Ask the insurer in writing for its inspection date and keep the reply.

The days after damage feel like a race, and it's easy to fix the wrong thing first. Many homeowners lose part of their claim because the evidence is gone before anyone documents it, but you can protect both your home and your claim with the right first steps.
