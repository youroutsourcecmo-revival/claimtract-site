Should I Cash My Insurance Check If It Is Too Low?

Approved by Cedric Moore, Licensed Public Adjuster · Orlando, FL

THE SHORT ANSWER

In many cases, cashing a first check for undisputed damages does not stop you from asking for the rest of your claim money. Read the check and any letter with it first to make sure it doesn't say "full and final settlement," but a second set of eyes on the paperwork protects your right to supplement the claim. Have you already deposited the check, or is it still sitting on your kitchen counter?

When a check shows up for a fraction of your repair bill, it's smart to pause before walking into the bank. Homeowners hear stories about people signing away their rights by endorsing a low settlement check, but knowing what to look for removes the guesswork so you can get repairs started safely. Would you like to know which words on a check should make you stop?

Insurance companies generally pay the undisputed amount they agree they owe. Cashing that check gives you money to tarp, dry out or order materials while the rest of the claim is negotiated, but if the memo line or letter says cashing it settles the entire claim, depositing it can lock you out of future payments. Have you checked the front and back of the check for release language?

Another wrinkle comes when you still have a mortgage on your home in Orlando. Your mortgage company's name will almost always be on the check next to yours, so you can't cash it without their endorsement, but knowing their inspection and draw rules ahead of time keeps your money from getting stuck in red tape. Is your mortgage lender listed on the check?

Some homeowners hold onto a low check for months, thinking it sends a message. Leaving a check in a drawer doesn't make the carrier raise its offer, and checks can expire after ninety or one hundred eighty days, but depositing an undisputed payment while telling the carrier in writing that you dispute the balance keeps your money working for you. Are you worried that cashing it tells the insurer you agree with their number?

If your check came without a detailed breakdown, don't rush to spend it before seeing the math. You have the right to see every room, measurement and price the desk adjuster used, but once you have the full estimate, you can deposit the undisputed portion and submit a supplemental claim for everything they missed. Did the insurance company send you the full line-by-line estimate?

What to do next

  1. Read the memo line, the back of the check, and every page of the letter in the envelope.
  2. Look for phrases like "full and final payment," "release of all claims" or "accord and satisfaction" before signing.
  3. Keep the check stub and take a clear photo of the check for your claim folder.
  4. If your mortgage company is on the check, call its loss draft department to learn the endorsement steps.
  5. Have a licensed public adjuster review the Statement of Loss so you can file a supplement for the unpaid balance.

A small first check doesn't mean your claim is over, as long as you handle the paperwork carefully from day one. Insurance companies issue partial payments every day, and many homeowners are too nervous or confused to ask for the rest, but you can use that first check to protect your home while I work for the full amount owed under your policy.
