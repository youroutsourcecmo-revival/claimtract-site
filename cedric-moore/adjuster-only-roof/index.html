What To Do When the Adjuster Only Looked at My Roof

Approved by Cedric Moore, Licensed Public Adjuster · Orlando, FL

THE SHORT ANSWER

When an adjuster only inspects your roof and skips the inside of your home, your estimate can miss hidden water damage, wet insulation and stained ceilings. Field adjusters often follow narrow instructions from a desk adjuster, but you can request a re-inspection and submit full interior documentation to get every damaged room included. Did the adjuster who visited ever ask to step inside?

It happens after almost every windstorm in Orlando: a ladder goes up, someone walks the shingles for ten minutes, and they drive away without knocking on your front door. Homeowners feel brushed off when an inspector ignores the inside of the house, but understanding why they skip the interior shows you how to get those rooms added to your claim. Would you like to know why the field adjuster stayed outside?

Many field adjusters are independent contractors given instructions from the desk adjuster before they arrive. If your first phone report only mentioned roof damage, the inspection may have been set up for the roof only, but wind-driven rain and lifted shingles often push water into your attic and down into your drywall. Have you noticed musty smells, bubbled paint or faint yellow rings on your ceilings since the storm?

Even when water hasn't soaked through to your living room ceiling yet, wet attic insulation and roof decking cause serious problems in Florida's heat and humidity. Wet insulation trapped above your drywall invites mold and wood rot, but documenting moisture in the attic shows the storm damage goes deeper than a few missing shingles. Has anyone climbed into your attic with a flashlight since the storm?

Water also travels sideways along rafters and wires, so a leak over the garage can show up as warped flooring or swollen baseboards two rooms away. A ten-minute roof walk will never connect those dots, but a complete room-by-room inspection ties your interior damage back to the storm opening on your roof. Are you seeing water spots or warped trim in rooms that aren't right under the roof damage?

If you already got an estimate that only lists roof items, your claim file is incomplete. Insurance companies won't pay to paint ceilings, replace wet insulation or dry out drywall unless someone puts those measurements and photos in front of the desk adjuster, but a complete interior and exterior scope gives them what they need to reopen the file and account for the whole house. Would you like help documenting the inside of your home before the damage gets worse?

What to do next

  1. Walk through every room in daylight and look closely at ceilings, crown molding, window frames and closets.
  2. Take clear, well-lit photos and videos of every stain, crack or swollen baseboard you find.
  3. If it's safe, check your attic hatch for damp insulation or dark water trails on the rafters.
  4. Write down the date you first noticed each spot so your file has a clear timeline.
  5. Call a licensed public adjuster for a full top-to-bottom inspection.

You pay for coverage on your entire home, not just the shingles on top of it. Letting an incomplete inspection stand can leave you paying out of pocket for drywall, paint and insulation months from now, but a thorough interior inspection puts the full truth of your loss on the record.
