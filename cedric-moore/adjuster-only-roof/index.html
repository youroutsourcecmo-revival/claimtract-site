THE SHORT ANSWER

When an adjuster only inspects one area, like the roof or the room with the stain, your estimate can miss hidden water damage, wet insulation and damage in the rooms next door. Field adjusters often follow narrow instructions from a desk adjuster, but you can request a re-inspection and submit full documentation to get every damaged area included. Did the adjuster who visited look at your whole home?

It happens on claims every week in Orlando: someone measures one spot for ten minutes and drives away without looking at the rest of the house. Homeowners feel brushed off when an inspector ignores most of the damage, but understanding why they skip it shows you how to get those areas added to your claim. Would you like to know why the inspection was so narrow?

Many field adjusters are independent contractors given instructions from the desk adjuster before they arrive. If your first phone report only mentioned the roof or one room, the inspection may have been set up for that area only, but water travels, and damage almost never stays where it started. Have you noticed musty smells, bubbled paint, soft flooring or faint yellow rings since the damage happened?

Even when water hasn't shown up on a ceiling or wall yet, wet insulation, subfloors and cabinets cause serious problems in Florida's heat and humidity. Hidden moisture invites mold and wood rot, but documenting it shows the damage goes deeper than what the first inspector saw. Has anyone checked inside your attic, walls or cabinets since this started?

Water travels along rafters, pipes and subfloors, so a leak in one room can show up as warped flooring or swollen baseboards two rooms away. A ten-minute look at one spot will never connect those dots, but a complete room-by-room inspection ties all of the damage back to its source. Are you seeing water spots or warped trim in rooms that seem unrelated?

If you already got an estimate that only lists one area, your claim file is incomplete. Insurance companies won't pay to replace flooring, dry out walls or rebuild cabinets unless someone puts those measurements and photos in front of the desk adjuster, but a complete scope gives them what they need to reopen the file and account for the whole house. Would you like help documenting the rest of your home before the damage gets worse?

What to do next

  1. Walk through every room in daylight and look closely at ceilings, floors, baseboards, cabinets and closets.
  2. Take clear, well-lit photos and videos of every stain, crack, soft spot or swollen baseboard you find.
  3. If it's safe, check your attic, under sinks and inside cabinets for damp spots or dark water trails.
  4. Write down the date you first noticed each spot so your file has a clear timeline.
  5. Call a licensed public adjuster for a full top-to-bottom inspection.

You pay for coverage on your entire home, not just the one spot someone looked at. Letting an incomplete inspection stand can leave you paying out of pocket for repairs months from now, but a thorough inspection puts the full truth of your loss on the record.
