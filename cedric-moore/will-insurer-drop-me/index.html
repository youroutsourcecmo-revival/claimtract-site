THE SHORT ANSWER

Hiring a public adjuster is not, by itself, a reason an insurer can give for dropping you. Rates and renewals are driven by the loss, the condition of the home and the insurer's own underwriting, not by who helps you present the claim, and Florida limits when an insurer can cancel or nonrenew a home damaged by a covered peril before repairs are done. Is the worry about this claim, or about what happens at your next renewal?

A lot of homeowners hesitate to get help because they're afraid the insurance company will punish them for it. That fear is understandable when you've heard stories of policies not being renewed, but the claim itself is what the insurer looks at, not whether you had someone in your corner presenting it. Would it ease your mind to know what actually drives a nonrenewal?

Insurers price and renew policies based on risk: the age and condition of the roof, past losses, the location and their own business decisions. Filing a claim can affect your future premium whether or not you hire anyone, but having a licensed professional document the damage accurately doesn't add a second strike to your record. Have you already reported the claim to your insurance company?

Florida also has rules about canceling a damaged home. In general, an insurer can't cancel or nonrenew a policy on a home damaged by a covered peril until a period after repairs are completed, with limited exceptions. The details depend on your policy and the situation, but the law is written to keep families from losing coverage in the middle of a claim. Are repairs on your home finished, or still in progress?

Some homeowners try to keep the claim small, hoping it keeps the insurer happy. Leaving damage out doesn't protect your renewal, and missed damage often turns into a bigger problem later, but documenting everything the first time gives you the money to fix the home properly, which is what makes it insurable going forward. Did the insurer's adjuster look at every damaged area?

If you're worried about the market overall, that's a fair concern in Florida right now. Premiums have risen for many homeowners regardless of their claims, but you can ask your agent directly what factors affect your renewal and keep your home documented and maintained so it stays in good standing. Would it help to talk through what's in your policy before you decide?

What to do next

  1. Report the claim to your insurer if you haven't already.
  2. Ask your agent in writing which factors affect your renewal.
  3. Keep photos, receipts and repair records in one folder.
  4. Finish repairs promptly once the claim is paid, and keep the invoices.
  5. Have a licensed public adjuster review your policy before you settle.

Fear of losing coverage keeps a lot of families from asking for what they're owed. Insurers count on that hesitation, but you can protect your home and your claim at the same time by getting the damage documented right.
