How Much Does a Public Adjuster Cost in Florida?

Approved by Cedric Moore, Licensed Public Adjuster · Orlando, FL

THE SHORT ANSWER

Most public adjusters in Florida work on contingency, so you pay nothing upfront and the fee is a percentage of the claim money recovered. Florida law caps that percentage: 10 percent on claims from a declared state of emergency during the year after the declaration, and 20 percent on other claims, with the fee spelled out in a written contract you can cancel within the period the law allows. Do you know whether your damage came from a declared emergency like a named storm?

Most homeowners never ask what a public adjuster costs until they're already worried about money. That makes sense after a loss, when every dollar feels spoken for, but the fee is set by law and written down before any work starts, so nothing about it has to be a mystery. Would it help to see exactly how the percentage is figured?

A public adjuster in Florida is paid from the claim money the insurance company pays, not from your pocket today. If the insurer pays nothing, a contingency fee is a percentage of nothing, but if the work brings in more money, the fee comes out of that larger amount and you still keep the larger share. Have you already received any payment on this claim?

Florida caps the fee. For claims from an event the Governor declared a state of emergency, the cap is 10 percent for claims made during the year after the declaration, and for other claims the cap is 20 percent. Some adjusters charge less than the cap, but no licensed public adjuster in Florida can charge more, and the percentage has to be in the written contract you sign. Was your damage from a named storm or another declared emergency?

Another rule protects homeowners who already got a check. If you hire a public adjuster to reopen or supplement a claim that was partly paid, the fee can only be charged on the new money recovered, not on what the insurer already paid you. That means hiring help late doesn't cost you a cut of the first check, but it does mean the sooner the full damage is documented, the more of the work happens on the first pass. Has your insurer already sent you a payment you think is too low?

Florida contracts for public adjusters must also tell you how to cancel. You get a window to cancel without penalty after signing, and the contract has to spell it out in large bold type, but the best protection is still reading the fee line with the adjuster before you sign anything. Would you like to go through a fee agreement line by line before you decide?

What to do next

  1. Ask any public adjuster for the exact percentage in writing before signing.
  2. Check whether your loss is tied to a declared state of emergency.
  3. If you already received a payment, confirm the fee applies only to new money recovered.
  4. Read the cancellation section of the contract and note the deadline.
  5. Verify the adjuster's Florida license on the Department of Financial Services website.

Paying for help after a loss can feel like one more bill you don't need. Many homeowners settle for less because they assume a public adjuster costs more than they'll recover, but you can see the math up front, in writing, before you commit to anything.
