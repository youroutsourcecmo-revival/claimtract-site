How Long Do I Have to File or Reopen a Claim in Florida?

THE SHORT ANSWER

Under current Florida law, notice of a new or reopened property claim generally must be given to your insurer within one year of the date of loss, and a supplemental claim within 18 months. Your policy may set its own rules, and older losses may fall under earlier deadlines, so check the date of loss and your policy right away. Do you know the exact date the damage happened?

Many homeowners think they have years to deal with a claim. Florida shortened its deadlines in recent years, and waiting can cost you the right to be paid at all, but knowing the dates up front gives you time to do it right. Do you know when your damage happened?

For a new claim or a reopened claim, Florida law generally requires notice to the insurer within one year after the date of loss. That's a short window if the damage was hidden, like a slow leak, but reporting as soon as you find damage keeps your rights intact while you figure out the rest. Have you already reported the damage to your insurance company?

A supplemental claim is for additional damage from the same loss that the insurer already adjusted. Florida generally allows notice of a supplemental claim within 18 months of the date of loss, which gives you time to find what was missed, but the clock starts on the date of loss, not the date you got your first check. Did the insurer's estimate leave out damage you can see now?

Your policy matters too. Some policies have their own notice terms, and losses from before the law changed may follow older rules, but reading the conditions section of your policy tells you what you agreed to and what you need to send. Do you have a copy of your full policy, not just the declarations page?

Deadlines are also the reason not to wait on a low payment. If you think the first check was too low, the time to document the missing damage is now, while the evidence is fresh and the 18 months are still running, but a written, organized supplement is much stronger than a phone call. Would you like help figuring out exactly where your deadlines fall?

What to do next

  1. Write down the date of loss and how you know it.
  2. Report the damage to your insurer in writing as soon as you find it.
  3. Mark one year and 18 months from the date of loss on your calendar.
  4. Request a full certified copy of your policy.
  5. Have a licensed public adjuster review any payment you think is too low before the window closes.

Losing a claim to a calendar is one of the most painful things that can happen to a homeowner. Deadlines in Florida are shorter than most people think, but you can protect your rights today just by knowing your dates and reporting in writing.
