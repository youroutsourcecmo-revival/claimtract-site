Who Works For You

Orlando, FL · By Cedric Moore, Licensed Public Adjuster

When a storm hits your home in Orlando, your insurance company opens a file and puts a team of professionals to work right away. Most homeowners assume the person who shows up with a ladder is there to make sure they get paid fairly, but that person works for the carrier, and you have the legal right to bring in your own licensed representative. Do you know who is actually looking out for your side of the claim before you sign anything?

Filing a claim feels like a simple paperwork process until the first repair estimate comes back way lower than what a local contractor charges. You want to trust that your policy will cover the damage you paid premiums to protect, but insurance contracts are written in dense language that makes it easy to miss money you are owed unless someone reads every page for you. Have you had a chance to look at your policy's actual replacement rules, or would you like me to walk through them with you?

The moment you call in a loss and get a claim number, your insurance company puts four different layers of people on your file. You only ever see one of them in person, but knowing how the other three operate behind the scenes helps you understand why claims get delayed or underpaid. Would you like to know what happens to your file after the inspection truck leaves your driveway?

1

The field adjuster

The one you meet

They are often an independent contractor hired for the day who may work for several different insurance companies at once. They are usually friendly and polite while measuring and taking photos, but they do not have the authority to decide what you get paid. Have you already had someone walk your property, or are you still waiting for that first visit?

2

The desk adjuster

The one you never meet

They read your claim notes first, tell the field adjuster what to look at, often just the roof, so nobody checks your ceilings or walls, and review the final report line by line. But you can submit your own complete proof of all interior and exterior damage so nothing gets left off the table. Did the person who inspected your home check inside your attic and ceilings?

3

The supervisor

Watches the numbers

The manager who watches the claim numbers and keeps total payouts in line. A desk adjuster's authority can stop at a certain dollar limit, but a documented, line-by-line estimate backed by your policy language can push your claim to a proper review. Are you worried your claim is stuck waiting on someone higher up?

4

The legal department

Waiting in the background

As long as a homeowner accepts the first check and nobody pushes back on a low estimate or a partial denial, the lawyers never have to get involved. But you do not have to accept an unfair settlement just to avoid a fight when a licensed advocate can negotiate the numbers for you.

What is a public adjuster?

How a public adjuster is paid

Most public adjusters charge a percentage of what the claim recovers, and some charge a flat or hourly fee. Fees vary by state and by adjuster, and the fee comes out of the settlement. Ask any adjuster to put the fee in writing before you sign anything.

Guides for homeowners

The first 48 hours after damage
  • Get everyone safe. If the house isn’t safe, leave it.
  • Stop more damage if you can do it safely: tarp the roof, shut off the water, dry what you can.
  • Take photos and video of everything before you clean up. Wide shots first, then close-ups.
  • Keep damaged materials if you can. Don’t throw them out until the damage is documented.
  • Save every receipt: tarps, fans, water removal, a hotel, meals.
  • Report the claim to your insurance company and write down your claim number.
  • Don’t sign anything you don’t understand.

Questions homeowners ask

Why is my first insurance check way smaller than my roofer's estimate?

Insurance companies usually pay claims in two parts, starting with the depreciated value of your damaged property rather than the full replacement cost. They can also leave out local building code upgrades, labor rates, or hidden water damage that a contractor sees right away. But you may be able to recover the held-back money and add missed items once the full scope of repairs is properly documented and submitted.

I review your insurer's payout sheet side by side with real local repair costs to find what they left out.

The adjuster only spent ten minutes on my roof and never came inside. Is that normal?

Field adjusters often carry heavy caseloads after a Florida storm and may only have instructions from the desk adjuster to look at one specific area. But an incomplete inspection does not close the door on your claim when you bring in thorough photo and moisture documentation.

Should I cash the first check the insurance company sent me?

Many homeowners freeze when a check arrives because they worry that depositing it means the claim is finished forever. In many cases, cashing an initial undisputed payment does not stop you from asking for the rest of what you are owed, but always check the memo line and the letter that came with it to make sure it does not say "full and final settlement."

My claim was denied for "wear and tear." Can I still fight it?

Insurers frequently blame roof leaks and missing shingles on old age or normal wear and tear, especially on Florida roofs over ten years old. A denial letter feels like the final word, but storm wind and hail leave damage patterns that differ from simple aging when documented correctly.

How much does it cost to hire a public adjuster in Florida?

Public adjusters usually work on a contingency fee, which means you pay nothing upfront and pay a percentage of the claim money recovered. Fees vary by claim and by whether the state has declared an emergency, but Florida law caps what we can charge and requires that you get the fee in writing before signing any contract.

Should I make repairs before the insurance adjuster comes to look at my house?

You have a duty under your policy to protect your home from further damage, like tarping a leaking roof or extracting standing water. Tearing out drywall or replacing a roof before the insurer sees it can hurt your claim, but clear photos and videos taken before emergency dry-out protect both your house and your claim.

What is "recoverable depreciation" and how do I get it back?

When an insurer calculates your loss, it subtracts a percentage for the age and condition of your roof or flooring and holds that money back until the work is done. That held-back amount is called recoverable depreciation, but if you have a Replacement Cost Value policy, you can claim it once you show proof that repairs are underway or done.

I track your depreciation holdbacks and submit the right invoices so the carrier releases the rest.

Can't my roofer or general contractor just handle the insurance claim for me?

Good contractors know how to rebuild your house and can give you a fair price for their trade. Under Florida law, a contractor cannot negotiate your insurance settlement unless they also hold a public adjuster license, but working alongside a public adjuster lets your contractor focus on building while I handle the policy and pricing.

How long do I have to file or reopen a storm claim in Florida?

Florida has strict deadlines for reporting a new claim and shorter windows for a supplemental claim when hidden damage shows up later. Waiting too long can bar you from collecting money you are owed, but acting quickly preserves your right to reopen an underpaid file.
