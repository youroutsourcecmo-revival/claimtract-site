What is a public adjuster?

A public adjuster is a licensed insurance professional you hire to handle your insurance claim. I'm Cedric, and I work for you, the property owner, not for the insurance company.

01

Read your policy

I go through your coverage, endorsements and exclusions so you know what to ask for.

02

Document the damage

I inspect the property, photograph and measure the loss, list what was damaged, and build the repair estimate.

03

Negotiate your claim

I present your claim to the insurance company, answer what it asks for, and work toward a fair settlement for you.

How a public adjuster is paid

Most public adjusters charge a percentage of what the claim recovers, and some charge a flat or hourly fee. Fees vary by state and by adjuster, and the fee comes out of the settlement. Ask any adjuster to put the fee in writing before you sign anything.

Guides for homeowners

The first 48 hours after damage
  • Get everyone safe. If the house isn’t safe, leave it.
  • Stop more damage if you can do it safely: tarp the roof, shut off the water, dry what you can.
  • Take photos and video of everything before you clean up. Wide shots first, then close-ups.
  • Keep damaged materials if you can. Don’t throw them out until the damage is documented.
  • Save every receipt: tarps, fans, water removal, a hotel, meals.
  • Report the claim to your insurance company and write down your claim number.
  • Don’t sign anything you don’t understand.

Hurricane season checklist
  • Find your policy and save a copy on your phone and in your email.
  • Know your hurricane deductible. It’s often a percentage of your home’s value, not a flat amount.
  • Walk through your house and record a video of every room, opening closets and drawers.
  • Photograph your roof, windows and the outside of the house from every side.

What should you not say to a claims adjuster?

Stick to facts. Do not guess at the cause or the age of the damage, do not say it is probably nothing, and do not agree to anything you do not understand. You can say you will answer in writing after you have reviewed your policy. Keep every email and letter, and note the date and time of every call. This is general information, not legal advice.

Why is my first check smaller than the estimate?

Many policies pay actual cash value first, which is the repair cost minus depreciation and your deductible, and pay the rest after repairs are done. Terms and deadlines vary by policy, so read the letter that came with the check.

The insurance estimate is too low and no contractor will take the job. What now?

That is common. Estimates and real repair costs can differ for many reasons, such as missed items, labor, and materials. A public adjuster builds a line-by-line estimate of the true cost to repair and presents the difference to the insurance company in writing.

Should I repair anything before the insurance adjuster comes out?

Protect your home from further damage first, such as tarping a roof or drying out water. Take photos and keep receipts, and keep damaged materials if you can. Do not start permanent repairs until the damage is documented.

If I cash the insurance check, is the claim over?

Not always, but read the check, the letter and anything you are asked to sign first. A partial payment is often not the final one. When in doubt, ask before you cash it.

Who does a public adjuster work for?

A public adjuster represents you, the policyholder, not the insurance company, and not your roofer or contractor. Public adjusters are licensed by the state and are generally held to a duty to act in good faith for the person they represent.

Can my contractor handle my insurance claim?

Contractors are good at repairs, but handling the insurance claim is a separate role. In many places, negotiating a claim for someone requires a public adjuster or attorney license. A contractor and a public adjuster can work well together when each stays in their own lane.
